03 · No grid

No pixels. Ever.

Even at 480i or 480p, a CRT shows no visible pixel grid, because the beam's Gaussian spot blends into the phosphor. That's why CRT-VR's foveation feels invisible: away from where you look, the image gets softer, never blocky. Your peripheral vision is soft anyway, so the illusion never breaks.

Same periphery · two displaysIllustrative · move to look around

Two copies of the same scene, foveated around your gaze. On the grid panel the low-resolution periphery shows square blocks. On CRT-VR the same low-resolution periphery is soft and smooth, with no visible pixels.

04 · Ahead of its time

The industry left it behind. It was simply ahead of its time.

CRT was called bulky, inefficient, retro. What no one saw was the medium it was made for: a tiny screen right in front of your eye, where size stops mattering, and motion clarity, true black and the absence of a pixel grid matter most. Then VR arrived.

The timeline starts with Braun's tube in 1897 and runs through the oscilloscope and radar, TV, Trinitron, Chromatron, the Watchman flat CRT, the vector arcade and Vectrex, the flat-panel takeover in the 2000s, and the CRT holdouts in esports and retro gaming, ending at CRT-VR.

Act 02

Gaze-Native

Act 02

Gaze-Native

The beam goes where your eye goes.

Hardware foveation

Foveated in the hardware.

A grid panel draws every pixel, every frame, whether or not you're looking. CRT-VR's beam follows your gaze. Full detail where you look, interlaced detail around it, and a fast, low-res edge tuned for catching motion.

Skip-Scan

Skip what you can't see.

The beam reads a normal framebuffer in a raster scan-line pass, and uses vector movement to jump straight over the dots foveation skips, without dwelling on them. No vector rendering is involved.

Acuity

Built for how eyes actually see.

Your sharpest vision covers about 2°. Beyond that, acuity falls off fast. CRT-VR's resolution map follows the same curve, so no beam time is spent on detail your eye can't use.

Three zones

Three zones. One seamless image.

Fovea: Full 24K-class density, drawn every frame.

Mid-zone: Lower resolution, interlaced, alternating lines each frame.

Edge: Low resolution, never interlaced, and built for spotting motion.

Flat panels

Why flat panels can't do this.

On OLED and LCD, foveation only saves GPU work. The panel still drives every pixel at a fixed refresh, and the periphery turns to blocks. On CRT-VR, every skipped dot is beam time saved, and that time becomes refresh rate. Precedent: Varjo's dual-panel approach.

Eye freedom

Look with your eyes, not your neck.

In today's VR you turn your head because only the center is sharp. CRT-VR's Gaze-Cone eyepiece is uniformly sharp across the ±50° cone your eye can rotate through, and a Periphery Ring carries the view out to 160° per eye, so you can glance the way you do in real life.

Presets

Quality. Balanced. Velocity.

Quality, Balanced and Velocity trade periphery detail for refresh rate, from 226 Hz to 800+ Hz.

Pipeline

Foveated end to end.

Gaze in, foveated frame in, over Intel Eventide iDP. The GPU renders only what the beam will draw, cutting link bandwidth by about 10–20×.

Act 03

Velocity

Act 03

Velocity

One screen. Thousands of refreshes a second.

Vari-refresh

The less it draws, the faster it gets.

CRT-VR refreshes the whole screen in a single raster pass. Where detail isn't needed, the beam takes bigger jumps, so the frame finishes sooner and the screen refreshes faster. 2,400+ Hz when you look into a corner, where the high-res region is clipped to about a quarter.

† Figures from the NewGen scan model. Design targets, to be recomputed at 24K.

Motion-adaptive

When everything moves, go all speed.

Resolution matters less when everything is moving. During fast motion, like a whip-pan or a quick head turn, Motion-Adaptive Foveated Rendering drops the entire screen, fovea included, to stage-3 foveation. With the coarsest skips everywhere, the whole-screen refresh rockets past 2,400 Hz even at the mildest setting, and beyond 3,000 Hz at the most aggressive. When motion settles, full three-zone foveation and every bit of detail come straight back. It all happens in a split second. As the motion slows and its natural blur fades, your fovea snaps back to full resolution, so you never notice the switch.

Traveling-Wave Deflection

Steering at the speed of the beam.

Segmented MEMS plates whose field travels with the electrons, for GHz-class slew.

Charge-Dense Dwell

Half a nanosecond per dot.

Schottky current headroom cuts pixel dwell from about 4 ns to about 0.5 ns.

Open-Loop Slew

Jump now. Correct next pass.

The beam doesn't wait to settle. Hexa-Index measures where it landed and corrects it on the next pass.

Nanosecond QDs

No afterglow. Ever.

Quantum dots fade in nanoseconds, so even at 2,400+ Hz there's no ghosting.

C and Y push the boundary out into the cyan-greens and golds no RGB triangle reaches. M sits on the purple line, the straight edge between the blue and red peaks, so it adds no xy area. Its job is Puretone: magentas from one subpixel. Complementary pairs face each other through white: R↔C, Y↔B, G↔M.

Colorimetric: spectral locus and primaries computed from the CIE 1931 2° observer (1 nm). Each primary is modeled as a 20 nm FWHM QD emission line; M is a two-peak B+R blend. Areas are in xy. Your screen cannot show colors outside its own gamut, so the fill is a desaturated preview.

Meet the six

Meet the six.

Blue ~450 nm: deep enough for a wide blue corner without sliding into dim violet.

Cyan ~490 nm: fills the cyan-green bulge where three-primary displays lose the most.

Green ~520 nm: near the tip of the spectral horseshoe, for the widest green reach.

Yellow ~575 nm: a bright, efficient tone that pushes the yellow edge and renders pure yellow alone.

Red ~635 nm: deep red without spending energy where the eye barely sees.

M: a true two-peak magenta, kept magenta so Puretone draws pure magenta from one subpixel.

Puretone™

Puretone™. The purest way to every color.

Every subpixel can work alone. Pure cyan lights only cyan. CRT-VR always builds a color from the fewest, closest pure tones, so color stays clean and power stays low. Only when you need more brightness do other tones join in.

The rule: each colour is rendered with the closest pure tone, or the fewest tones that hit it exactly. The Gun Shootout  · four candidates, one survivorElectron source trade study

Select a candidate. Decision is final unless reopened; ratings are qualitative engineering-judgment scores from the trade study, not measurements.

CHOSEN — THE SEM/TEM COLUMN SOURCE

ZrO/W tip at ~1800 K with strong extraction field. 2–4 orders of magnitude more current density than thermionic; nm-scale coherent virtual source; thermally self-cleaning — adsorbed gas bakes off continuously, so emission is stable for years, not hours. Forgiving vacuum requirement, non-toxic materials, <1 W heat plausible.

Consequence: the gun is never the brightness bottleneck (phosphor saturation is), and spot size carries 3+ orders of magnitude headroom over the ~2 µm triangle side at 24K on 4″.

TIER 2 — BEST RAW EFFICIENCY, WORST HABITS

CNT / Spindt / nanodiamond cold cathodes: best raw efficiency and instant-on. But emission current drifts as gas molecules adsorb onto the tip — the classic cold-FE reliability problem — demanding ~10⁻¹⁰ torr and periodic tip flashing.

Manageable with modern getter technology, but it trades away the one property a consumer display must have: set-and-forget stability. Kept on the bench as the fallback if Schottky heater power ever becomes the binding constraint.

Spread model: a power law through 0.1 µm at 2.5 kV and 1 µm at 10 kV (exponent 1.66, Kanaya–Okayama-like). At 2 kV the beam spreads only ~0.1 µm in the phosphor, far inside a ~2 µm subpixel. Wraparound Optics

Made for VR. Before VR existed.

Cone

Born a cone.

A flat panel has to stretch a rectangle around your eye, which is where distortion and the screen-in-a-box feel come from. A CRT's light already leaves as a cone. CRT-VR's optics simply turn that cone around to wrap your eye. The panel the industry tried to leave behind was simply ahead of its time.

Gaze-Cone

Sharp across the cone your eye sweeps.

A curved-field aspheric eyepiece per eye, uniformly sharp across the ±50° cone the eye can rotate through, with softening only in peripheral vision.

Beyond the cone, a thin Periphery Ring covers 55–80°, giving 160° per eye, where your vision is peripheral anyway.

First lens

One less lens.

The dome faceplate doubles as the first optical element.

Tiers

Glass worthy of the image.

All tiers use a fixed deterministic spacing with no pancake folds, so there are no ghosts, no sweet-spot loss and almost no brightness loss.

Sony (entry panels): Sony-quality aspherical glass.

Sony G (mid-range panels): G-series elements and coatings.

Sony G Master (high-end panels): XA extreme-aspherical elements, Nano AR Coating II and the full G Master coating stack.

ZEISS (select flagship panels): branded "G Master with ZEISS T*", in the same style as Sony's ZEISS-badged lenses. It is the full G Master relay with ZEISS T* coating, for the very top of the line.

Relay

Exploded relay

Pulled apart from the panel side: the faceplate as first lens, the Gaze-Cone eyepiece for each eye, and the Periphery Ring that carries the view out to 160° per eye. Use the slider to pull the parts apart.

Field curvature

Edge to edge.

The curved screen cancels the optics' field curvature, so it stays sharp right out to the edges.

CA pre-warp

Fringe-free, in hardware.

Each of the six beams pre-distorts its own primary, so the relay's color fringing cancels in hardware. Lens distortion pre-warp comes free with skip-scan.

Pupil swim

The world never wobbles.

When your eye rotates, the pupil shifts by millimeters. The panel uses gaze coordinates to adjust its geometry every frame, so the world never wobbles.

Eyebox

Room for you.

A generous eyebox, room for glasses or a prescription insert, and IPD range.

Glare

Darkness, protected.

A Kurocoat-lined relay barrel and baffles keep veiling glare from lifting the black floor, which ties into the black-level budget.

Field of view

Your whole field of view.

Up to about 160° per eye, and 200°+ across both eyes.

† Stretch design targets.

No grid

No screen-door. No mura.

With no grid, there's nothing to see between pixels.

Moonshot

Next: focus that follows you.

A moonshot beyond the Gaze-Cone baseline: an eyepiece whose focus follows where your eye is looking. It is not part of the design study's baseline.

Burn-in

Burn-in, compensated.

Hexa-Index tracks every subpixel's aging and evens it out.

Vacuum

A vacuum that stays a vacuum.

Non-evaporable getters (NEG) keep the vacuum by soaking up gas over the panel's whole life, the same approach long-life vacuum tubes and FEDs use. Getter capacity against outgassing is open question №9.

Durability

Tough by shape.

The shallow cap bows into the vacuum, so it carries the vacuum load in tension, like a membrane, at about 1–2 MPa, far below strengthened glass's limit. Chemically strengthened glass and vacuum-side ribs outside the active area hold it.

Honesty

What we don't know yet.

  1. P0 Feasibility-Deciding №1

    Two miniature vacuum tubes + Gaze-Cone optics covering 160° per eye and 200°+ binocular FOV vs. 107–170 g for today's shipping microOLED panel and optics. A first design estimate puts the CRT-VR panel module at about 300 g per eye, but the full-stack mass is still open. This is where "smaller, thinner" is proven or dies. Inputs needed: tube envelope wall thickness at wafer-level-sealed scale, relay lens element count/mass, HV supply mass.

  2. P0 Feasibility-Deciding №2

    The actual PPI limit of the whole system. Model beam scatter at short throw + QD stripe pitch vs. cross-talk. Gun and MEMS deflection plates carry 100–1000× margin; this subsystem sets max resolution.

  3. P0 Feasibility-Deciding №3

    The 1,500 nit full-field, 4,000+ nit peak and 1,000+ nit at-eye targets hinge on QD-phosphor saturation/degradation limits under e-beam flux. Needs a literature review of cathodoluminescent QD data at minimum; ideally lab data.

  4. P1 Architecture-Completing №4

    Motion thresholds: which pan and head-turn speeds should push foveation to its most aggressive?

  5. P1 Architecture-Completing №5

    Real-time 6-channel conversion, metamerism-aware optimization (multiple valid drive combos per color — optimize for brightness/efficiency/power). Precedent: Sharp Quattron processing silicon. Lives in the VR render pipeline. Could be prototyped in software now.

  6. P1 Architecture-Completing №6

    Lock the RGBMCY primary wavelengths for maximum hexagon area vs. the spectral locus, considering QD linewidths and eye sensitivity (CIE 1931 / CIE 170-2 observer).

  7. P1 Architecture-Completing №7

    Vector scan-plan generation per frame: foveal density map → point list → deflection DAC waveform. Tier boundaries, temporal subsampling patterns for the midground. Also software-prototypable.

  8. P2 Productization №8

    Acceleration voltage choice, insulation, failure modes, regulatory pathway. Solvable, but real engineering.

  9. P2 Productization №9

    NEG getter capacity vs. outgassing over product life.

  10. P2 Productization №10

    Litho phosphor striping + MEMS electrostatic deflection plates + QD emitters + wafer vacuum sealing have never been combined in one device. Identify closest analog processes: FED post-mortems, MEMS bolometer lines, QD-OLED deposition.

  11. P2 Productization №11

    XCD-LED-style purity-vs-brightness crossover points; perceptual validation of high-luminance primary recruitment.
